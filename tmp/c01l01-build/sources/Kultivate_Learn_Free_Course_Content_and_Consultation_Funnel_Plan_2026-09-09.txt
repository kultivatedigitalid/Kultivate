[Normal] RESEARCH
[Heading 1] Benchmark findings
[Heading 2] 1. Acquisition.com / Alex Hormozi
[Normal] Acquisition.com provides free advanced training in multiple formats: videos, eBooks, guides, templates, downloads, courses, and a personalized Scaling Roadmap. Current course structure includes Offer Creation, Lead Generation, Scaling, and other business-growth material; the free material is substantial rather than only promotional teaser content.
[TABLE]
Observed pattern | What it means for Kultivate
Deep free education | It is acceptable to give away frameworks, checklists, and practical knowledge when the paid value is contextual execution.
Course-specific downloads | Use worksheets and scorecards as genuine learning tools and natural points for optional data capture.
Scaling Roadmap | A diagnostic can bridge generic education and personalized service without hard-selling too early.
Workshop requires qualification call | The conversion event occurs after the user understands the problem and needs personalized help.
Different courses solve different constraints | Do not make every Learn track repeat the same intro to SEO/web. Each track should answer a distinct buying problem.
[Normal] For example, the Offer Creation course visibly includes modules such as market selection, pricing/value, offer creation, bonuses, guarantees, scarcity/urgency, and naming; downloadable checklists are attached to several modules. The Lead Generation course covers lead magnets, early-client acquisition, content, cold outreach, paid ads, referral, employees, agencies, affiliates, and a roadmap. The Scaling course is structured by business stage. The key lesson is the funnel logic: free education → diagnostic/personalized roadmap → qualified call/workshop → deeper relationship.
[Heading 2] 2. Ahrefs Academy
[Normal] Ahrefs shows that high-value technical education can remain free and ungated. Its beginner SEO course currently presents 14 lessons across 5 modules with a stated total length of about 2 hours and no sign-up requirement. It covers SEO basics, keyword research, search intent, on-page SEO, link building, and technical SEO. For Kultivate, this supports a stronger lesson depth than the current two-lessons-per-series structure while still keeping the content accessible.
[Heading 2] 3. Growth.Design
[Normal] Growth.Design is useful as a format benchmark rather than a direct service-funnel benchmark. Its free UX case studies use story-driven breakdowns, while its paid masterclass explicitly uses a Learn → Practice → Apply sequence and includes scorecards, checklists, cheat sheets, examples, and case studies. For Kultivate, the relevant adaptation is Learn → Diagnose → Decide, with implementation handled by Kultivate.
[Heading 2] 4. Google Search Central and web.dev
[Normal] Google’s SEO Starter Guide frames SEO as helping search engines understand content and helping users find a site and decide whether to visit it. Google also emphasizes useful, original, current, people-first content. This supports making Learn genuinely educational rather than thin lead-generation pages. web.dev case studies also provide strong public examples for connecting technical website decisions to business outcomes: Tokopedia reported a 35% CTR increase and 8% conversion increase after performance work, while Rakuten 24 reported materially higher conversion and revenue per visitor in its Core Web Vitals case study. These are useful educational examples, not promises of equivalent results for Kultivate clients.

[List Bullet] Depth: two lessons per series is too light to establish strong expertise for a decision-maker.
[List Bullet] Audience: Owner, Procurement, IT Lead, and Marketing Lead need different framing and proof.
[List Bullet] Outcome framing: several existing lessons are technically valid but should connect more explicitly to business decisions, risk, revenue, and project readiness.
[List Bullet] Conversion: current end-state points to a service page; the stronger end-state is a contextual diagnostic/roadmap followed by free first consultation.
[List Bullet] Data: current learning analytics capture behavior, but not yet the buyer profile, business problem, urgency, or project intent.
[List Bullet] Case studies: portfolio proof should be complemented by educational case studies that reveal diagnosis, trade-offs, and reasoning.
[Normal] STRATEGY
[Heading 1] Operating principles for Learn
[Heading 3] 1. Knowledge can be free
[Normal] Explain what the problem is, why it matters, the framework, how to inspect it, trade-offs, and what good execution looks like.
[Heading 3] 2. Execution remains the service
[Normal] Research, data collection, prioritization, implementation, QA, stakeholder alignment, maintenance, and accountability are where Kultivate creates paid value.
[Heading 3] 3. Business problem before channel
[Normal] Course framing should begin with revenue, discovery, trust, conversion, technical risk, procurement risk, or migration risk — not with tool features.
[Heading 3] 4. Decision-makers, not practitioners
[Normal] Teach enough technical detail to make a good decision. Avoid turning Owner/Procurement into a full-time SEO specialist or developer.
[Heading 3] 5. Open learning, progressive data
[Normal] Do not gate the first lesson or basic course. Ask for data only when offering proportionally greater value: a template, scorecard, diagnostic, or personalized roadmap.
[Heading 3] 6. Evidence over hype
[Normal] Use real case studies, measurable examples, clear caveats, and explicit assumptions. Do not promise ranking, conversion, or revenue outcomes.
[Heading 3] 7. Every lesson must move the buyer
[Normal] A lesson should increase understanding, improve self-diagnosis, clarify a decision, or reveal an execution dependency.

[Normal] CREDIBILITY
[Heading 1] Case Study Learning System
[Normal] Portfolio proves that work exists; educational case studies prove how Kultivate thinks. Learn should therefore include a case-study layer that exposes diagnosis, evidence, choices, trade-offs, implementation constraints, and result interpretation.
[Heading 2] Case study structure
[TABLE]
Step | What to show
1. Business Context | Who the business serves, what the website/search function needed to support, and relevant constraints.
2. Problem | Observable business/user/technical problem, not a vague “website looked old”.
3. Evidence | Analytics, search data, technical findings, user observations, stakeholder input, or public source evidence.
4. Diagnosis | Why the problem is happening; distinguish symptom from root cause.
5. Options | Reasonable alternatives, including why “do nothing” or a smaller intervention may be valid.
6. Decision | What was prioritized and why.
7. Execution | What changed at strategy, content, UX, SEO, development, measurement, or process level.
8. Result | Observed outcome with timeframe and limitations. Never imply causality beyond the evidence.
9. Trade-off | What the chosen solution did not solve or what risk/cost was accepted.
10. Next Priority | What should be measured or improved next.

Internal Kultivate case: website friction → prioritization → targeted fixes | Website / UX | Use only after verified evidence; include before/after reasoning and limitations.
[Normal] BOUNDARY
[Heading 1] What to give away vs what Kultivate executes
[TABLE]
Rule of thumb / If the information helps the buyer understand, diagnose at a high level, evaluate a vendor, or make a better decision, give it away. If it requires business-specific research, access, implementation skill, stakeholder coordination, testing, or ongoing accountability, it is part of execution.
[TABLE]
Area | Teach openly | Execution Kultivate
SEO Strategy | Intent, demand mapping, page-type logic, prioritization principles, measurement concepts. | Keyword/prompt dataset, SERP/competitor research, page mapping, forecast assumptions, roadmap, implementation, reporting.
Technical SEO | Crawl/index concepts, common risks, owner checklist, migration principles. | Crawls, log/technical analysis, fixes, schema, redirects, validation, monitoring.
Content / Service Pages | Page anatomy, evidence, information hierarchy, intent alignment. | Research, expert interviews, copywriting, editing, design, CMS implementation, internal linking.
UX | Heuristics, friction categories, decision journey, CTA/form principles. | User research, analytics/session analysis, prototypes, usability tests, UI design, experiments.
Web Development | Architecture decision criteria, quality checklist, performance/accessibility concepts. | Technical discovery, system design, coding, integrations, deployment, QA, maintenance.
Procurement | Scope anatomy, vendor scorecard, acceptance-criteria examples, red flags. | Project-specific scope, technical due diligence, estimate, delivery plan, implementation.
AEO / GEO | Entity/evidence/answer-readiness concepts, measurement limitations. | Entity mapping, structured data, content changes, monitoring query set, data collection, iteration.
[Normal] FIRST-PARTY DATA
[Heading 1] Progressive data capture design
[Normal] The goal is not to maximize form fields. The goal is to understand who is learning, what problem they have, how close they are to buying, and which content created the transition. Data requests should be proportional to the value being delivered.
[TABLE]
Stage | User value | Data request | Why
Open Learn | Course, lesson, public case study | No form | Reduce friction and build trust.
Download | Worksheet, checklist, scorecard | Email + optional role | Useful low-friction relationship point; allow anonymous preview where feasible.
Diagnostic | Personalized score/result | Role, URL, primary challenge, goal | Creates segmentation and contextual recommendation.
Growth Roadmap | Prioritized next steps | Company, role, project intent, timeline, selected constraints | Qualification without asking for a full sales form too early.
Consultation | Human review and contextual recommendation | Contact details + project context required to schedule/prepare | Qualified handoff to business development / founder / specialist.
[Heading 2] Recommended profile fields
[TABLE]
Field | Use
Role | Owner/Founder; Procurement; IT/Tech Lead; Marketing/SEO; Other.
Website URL | Allows contextual audit/consultation and separates learners without an active site.
Primary challenge | Visibility; low inquiry; unclear positioning; UX/conversion; rebuild; migration; performance; vendor scope; AI search; other.
Primary goal | Increase qualified discovery; improve inquiry; reduce risk; prepare project; improve performance; understand next priority.
Current intent | Learning; evaluating options; planning a project; comparing vendors; ready to execute.
Timeline | No timeline; 3+ months; 1–3 months; <1 month / active project.
Company / team context | Use later in funnel, not before basic learning.
Email / contact | Request only when the user wants a saved result, roadmap, follow-up, or consultation.

[List Bullet] Any role + no active project → keep nurturing through Learn/Insights rather than forcing a sales conversation.

[Heading 2] Consultation positioning
[TABLE]
Recommended promise / The first consultation is free. Kultivate uses the conversation to understand the business problem, current context, and required work; pricing is then defined based on the pieces and scope actually needed.
[Normal] Do not position consultation as “the only place we reveal the answer.” The course should already explain the answer at framework level. Consultation adds context: what matters for this specific business, what should happen first, what can wait, what evidence is missing, and what execution is required.

[Normal] PRODUCTION
[Heading 1] Content production standard
[Heading 2] Lesson format — 6 to 12 minutes
[TABLE]
Segment | Purpose | Typical length
1. Business hook | State the decision/problem in the audience’s language; avoid generic “today we learn SEO”. | 30–60 sec
2. Context | Explain why the issue matters and when it does not matter. | 1–2 min
3. Framework | Teach the model, checklist, or decision rule. | 2–4 min
4. Example / case | Show a realistic or verified public/client example. | 1–3 min
5. Self-diagnostic | Give 2–5 questions the learner can apply immediately. | 1–2 min
6. Next step | Continue learning, use a worksheet, diagnose, or consult depending on intent. | 15–45 sec
[Heading 2] Content quality rules
[List Bullet] One lesson = one decision or mental model. Do not overload a lesson with a full practitioner workflow.
[List Bullet] Use the vocabulary of the target role. Owner copy should emphasize opportunity/risk; Procurement copy should emphasize scope/acceptance; IT copy can use technical language.
[List Bullet] Every claim with a number needs a source and contextual caveat.
[List Bullet] Never imply guaranteed ranking, conversion, revenue, AI citation, or performance outcomes.
[List Bullet] Show trade-offs. Good content should explain when Kultivate would not recommend a rebuild, SEO workstream, or additional complexity.
[List Bullet] Prefer founder/specialist explanations, natural teaching documentation, real diagrams, annotated website examples, and verified work over generic stock/AI visuals.
[List Bullet] Provide transcripts or written summaries for accessibility and SEO; structured lesson summaries also help users scan before watching.
[List Bullet] Update technical lessons periodically because search/AI products, browser metrics, analytics tools, and platform behavior can change.
[Heading 2] Worksheet / lead magnet standard
[List Bullet] Must be useful even if the user never contacts Kultivate.
[List Bullet] Maximum 1–3 pages for first version; designed to be completed in 5–15 minutes.
[List Bullet] Includes decision questions, score, next-action interpretation, and where expert help is actually needed.
[List Bullet] Should not require proprietary tools to be useful.
[List Bullet] Data capture is optional until the user wants to save results, receive a personalized interpretation, or continue to a Growth Roadmap.

[Normal] HANDOFF
[Heading 1] Implementation brief for the website team
[TABLE]
One-paragraph implementation brief / Revise Kultivate Learn on the staging branch from the current four-series/eight-lesson structure into a scalable education-to-consultation system built around six learning tracks and 36 planned lessons, while prioritizing the first 18 lessons across SEO for Business Growth, Website as a Business & Sales System, and How to Scope & Evaluate a Digital Project. Preserve open access to learning content, existing bilingual patterns, related-service navigation, and current funnel analytics, but add course completion, resource, diagnostic, roadmap, and consultation events; introduce progressive data capture through useful worksheets/scorecards and a role-aware Growth Priority diagnostic; and replace the current service-link-only end state with a contextual path from lesson → resource → diagnostic → personalized Growth Roadmap → free first consultation. The UI should clearly segment content by problem and audience without feeling like a generic LMS, keep founder-led teaching/evidence prominent, support transcripts/summaries, and ensure every CTA communicates that the knowledge is free while Kultivate handles contextual research, prioritization, design, development, technical implementation, QA, and ongoing optimization.
[Heading 2] Definition of done — content/funnel layer
[List Bullet] Course architecture supports at least the six defined tracks without hardcoding only four current series.
[List Bullet] Course/lesson metadata can store audience, business problem, learning outcome, resource CTA, diagnostic CTA, and related service.
[List Bullet] User can complete lessons without submitting personal data.
[List Bullet] Resource and diagnostic surfaces are optional progressive conversion points.
[List Bullet] Consultation CTA is attributable to course, lesson, role/diagnostic path where consented/technically appropriate.
[List Bullet] Bilingual ID/EN structure is preserved.
[List Bullet] Existing service pages remain the source of service scope truth; Learn should not invent services outside approved scope.
[List Bullet] Analytics events are documented and testable.
[List Bullet] Accessibility, responsive behavior, keyboard navigation, and reduced-motion behavior are included in QA.
[List Bullet] No fake founder imagery, fabricated results, fabricated case studies, or unsupported performance promises.
